# Module 1.1: Building Custom LLMs – Human Agent vs. LLM
### *Building Customized LLMs with OpenAI — Columbia Plus*

This unit serves as the technical opening of Module 1. Professor Johar introduces the strategic imperative for **customizing Large Language Models (LLMs)** and establishes a foundational comparison between the operational performance of human agents and their AI counterparts when tasked with domain-specific work.

---

## Learning Objectives
The primary goal of this session is to understand the performance trade-offs in modern business automation. Key focus areas include:
* **Customization Frameworks**: Why off-the-shelf models often fail in specialized domains and how customization bridges the gap.
* **Comparative Analysis**: Evaluating the speed, accuracy, and scalability of **Human Agents** versus **LLM Agents**.
* **Domain Specificity**: Understanding how specialized knowledge changes the "intelligence" requirement of a model.
* **Task Assignment Strategy**: Learning how to decide which business processes are best suited for LLM automation and which require human oversight.

---

## Key Concepts Covered

### **1. The Case for Customization**
Generic models possess broad knowledge but lack the "proprietary context" required for enterprise-level tasks. We explore:
* Fine-tuning for specific terminology and brand voice.
* Implementing guardrails to maintain professional standards.

### **2. Human vs. LLM Agents**
A direct comparison of operational characteristics:
* **Human Agents**: High empathy, nuanced reasoning, but limited by fatigue and linear scaling costs.
* **LLM Agents**: Instantaneous processing, 24/7 availability, and near-zero marginal cost per interaction, but prone to hallucinations if not properly grounded.

---

## Skills & Knowledge Acquired
* **Strategic Selection**: Ability to identify use cases where LLMs outperform humans (e.g., high-volume data retrieval).
* **Performance Benchmarking**: Understanding the metrics used to compare AI output with human subject matter experts.
* **Hybrid System Design**: Recognizing the value of "human-in-the-loop" systems for complex, high-stakes domains.
