# Module 1.7: Ethics, Policy, and Bias in AI

### *AI for Business — Columbia University*

This directory concludes the foundational module by examining the critical societal and legal implications of Artificial Intelligence. As AI systems take on more decision-making power, understanding the ethical framework and regulatory landscape is no longer optional for business leaders—it is a core requirement.

---

##  Learning Objectives

The primary goal of this unit is to navigate the "gray areas" of AI deployment. Key takeaways include:

* **Identifying Algorithmic Bias:** Understanding how historical data can lead to discriminatory outcomes in hiring, lending, and law enforcement.
* **The "Black Box" Problem:** Exploring the need for **Explainable AI (XAI)**—the ability to explain why a model made a specific decision.
* **Regulatory Landscape:** Preparing for emerging global policies like the EU AI Act and local data privacy laws.
* **Corporate Responsibility:** Establishing an internal ethical charter for AI use within your organization.

---

##  Key Concepts Covered

### **The Three Pillars of Responsible AI**

Executing an AI strategy requires balancing technical power with ethical safety:

1. **Fairness:** Detecting and mitigating bias in training datasets to ensure equitable treatment across different demographic groups.
2. **Transparency:** Moving away from "Black Box" models toward systems that provide interpretable results for stakeholders and regulators.
3. **Accountability:** Defining who is responsible when an AI system makes an error—the developer, the data provider, or the business owner?

### **AI Policy and Compliance**

The module discusses the evolving legal standards that businesses must follow:

* **Data Privacy:** Navigating GDPR, CCPA, and the ethical collection of user information.
* **Intellectual Property:** Understanding the legal challenges surrounding Generative AI and training data ownership.
* **Safety & Security:** Protecting AI models from "adversarial attacks" and ensuring they behave predictably in high-stakes environments like healthcare or finance.
