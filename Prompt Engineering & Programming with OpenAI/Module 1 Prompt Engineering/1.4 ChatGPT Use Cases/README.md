# Module 1.4: Building and Managing AI Teams

### *AI for Business — Columbia University*

Technology is only half the battle. This module focuses on the **human capital** required to successfully execute an AI strategy. You will learn how to structure teams, hire the right talent, and bridge the communication gap between "The Boardroom" and "The Server Room."

---

## Learning Objectives

The primary goal of this unit is to understand the organizational architecture of an AI-driven company. Key takeaways include:

* **The "Three Pillars" of Talent:** Distinguishing between Data Scientists, Data Engineers, and AI Product Managers.
* **Organizational Structures:** Comparing Centralized vs. Decentralized (Hub-and-Spoke) AI teams.
* **Hiring Strategies:** Identifying the core competencies needed for early-stage AI projects versus scaling phases.
* **Effective Leadership:** Learning how to manage technical expectations and define success for non-linear research projects.

---

## Key Concepts Covered

### **The AI Team Composition**

A successful AI initiative requires a cross-functional mix of roles. This module breaks down the responsibilities of:

* **The Data Scientist:** Focuses on algorithms and statistical modeling.
* **The Data Engineer:** Builds the "plumbing"—the pipelines that move and clean data.
* **The Machine Learning Engineer:** Focuses on the production and deployment of models.
* **The AI Translator:** The critical bridge role (often a Business Analyst or PM) who translates business needs into technical requirements.

### **Strategic Team Structure**

We explore how different company sizes approach AI:

* **Centralized:** A single "Center of Excellence" that serves all business units.
* **Decentralized:** AI talent is embedded directly within specific departments (Retail, Finance, etc.).
* **Hybrid (Hub-and-Spoke):** A central leadership team providing tools and standards, while specialized pods work on business-specific problems.
